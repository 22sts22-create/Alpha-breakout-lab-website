📈 Practice a real catalyst — how to get & load a CSV

Want to drill a real move instead of the demo? You need a price-history CSV of the stock's catalyst day. Daily bars are plenty to practice; here are the easiest free routes:

Option A — Stooq (free, no account, best for daily):

  1. Put this in your browser's address bar, swapping in your ticker:
    https://stooq.com/q/d/l/?s=cadl.us&i=d
  2. US tickers need the .us suffix (e.g. cadl.us, vktx.us).
  3. It downloads a .csv. Hit ⬆ Load CSV up top and pick it. Done.

Other sources:

  • Your broker — most let you export chart data to CSV.
  • Nasdaq / Yahoo Finance — historical-data pages offer a CSV download button.

The file needs columns Date, Open, High, Low, Close (Volume optional, but needed for VWAP & the Volume study). Your file stays in your browser; nothing is uploaded or stored.

Trading Terms — Quick Glossary

Everything you need to understand what the buttons, the studies, and the order book are doing.
Buy — An order to purchase shares. From Flat or Long it opens/adds to a long; while Short it covers (closes) the short. Marked B / S/B on the chart.
Sell — An order to sell shares. While Long it closes the long; from Flat it opens a short. Marked S / S/S on the chart.
Shares — Units of ownership in a company — your position size. "500 shares" means you're long (or short) 500 units; your profit is per-share move × shares.
Long — Buying a stock expecting it to go up. "Buy low, sell high." You own the shares.
Short (Sell Short) — Betting a stock goes DOWN. You sell shares you've borrowed, and profit if the price falls. Here: hit SELL while you're Flat. Marked S/S on the chart.
Cover (Buy to Cover) — Closing a short by buying the shares back. Cover lower than you shorted = profit; higher = loss. Marked S/B on the chart.
Flatten — Close your entire position at once — long or short — back to zero (Flat).
Bid / Ask — The Bid is the highest price buyers will pay; the Ask is the lowest price sellers will take. You buy at the Ask and sell at the Bid.
Spread — The gap between Bid and Ask. Tight spread = liquid, cheap to trade; wide spread = you lose more just entering and exiting.
Level 2 — The order book: all the resting buy and sell orders stacked by price, showing supply, demand, and the spread. (The one on this page is simulated for practice.)
CSV — A plain spreadsheet file (comma-separated values). Here it's a price-history file — columns Date, Open, High, Low, Close (Volume optional) — that you upload to trade a real stock's past moves.
Moving Average (SMA / EMA) — A line of the average price over the last N bars, smoothing out noise to show the trend. SMA weights every bar equally; EMA weights recent bars more, so it turns faster. Price above a rising MA = uptrend; below = downtrend.
VWAP — Volume-Weighted Average Price. The average price weighted by how much traded at each level, reset each day. Day traders treat it as the "fair value" line — holding above it is bullish, losing it is bearish.
Volume — How many shares traded in each bar (the bars along the bottom when the study is on). Big volume confirms a move is real; a spike on low volume often fizzles.
Average Cost — Your blended entry price across every share in your current position.
Unrealized P&L — Paper profit/loss on the position you still hold. It moves with every bar.
Realized P&L — Profit/loss you've locked in by closing out. This is the real score.
Equity — Your total account value: cash plus the current value of your position. The number that actually matters.
